How should an investor balance return and risk?

I analysed film performance to build two strategies for different risk appetites. Comparing average ROI with the spread of returns meant looking past the highest average to the uncertainty behind it.

The result was a clear trade-off. Comedy offered lower volatility; drama paired a higher average return with far wider swings.

From source data to a recommendation.

  1. Define the KPIs, prepare the data

    Set the KPIs: average ROI, its standard deviation, and ROI over risk. Combined the sources with lookup functions, cleaned and standardised the fields, and grouped budgets and runtimes for analysis.

  2. Compare the trade-offs

    Used PivotTables to compare genres, actors, directors, budgets, runtimes, content ratings and release months, always reading the average next to its spread.

  3. Communicate the decision

    Built the presentation around two strategies, and why a higher return does not automatically make an option the better one.

What the numbers can tell us.

The useful result is a way to compare trade-offs. Group averages show where to look next; a single film needs its own evidence.

Metric definitions and data scope
  • ROI proxy: (gross revenue − budget) / budget × 100. It leaves out unrecorded costs and revenue sharing, so it is not an investor's net return.
  • Risk: the sample standard deviation of ROI, in percentage points. It measures variability, not the probability of a loss.
  • ROI over risk: mean ROI divided by its standard deviation. A descriptive ratio, not a Sharpe ratio.
  • Scope: 3,725 source rows. The genre results reproduce the workbook's 2005–2015 analysis across available countries and languages.
  • Sample sizes: each of the six genres has more than 100 records. Actor and director comparisons use at least three films per person.
Limitations, and what I would test next
  • A few very profitable low-budget films can dominate an average. Medians, full distributions and outlier checks would make the comparison stronger.
  • The analysis does not show that an actor, director, release month or runtime causes higher returns.
  • The actor field and the small samples limit what can be said about star power.
  • A next iteration would standardise filters across views, validate the revenue definition and duplicates, and test the recommendations on a later period.
